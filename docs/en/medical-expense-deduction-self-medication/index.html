Medical expense deduction or the self-medication tax system: which saves you more tax?

Japan has two ways to deduct health spending from income, and you can use only one of them. The regular deduction for medical expenses covers what you paid above ¥100,000 (less if your income is low); the self-medication tax system covers eligible over-the-counter medicines above ¥12,000, if you had a health check or similar during the year. About this result

This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and simplified assumptions. Your actual tax depends on what you file and on your municipality. It does not replace the judgment of a tax accountant (zeirishi), the tax office or your city office. Check with a professional or the office in charge before any important decision.

It assumes you are a resident of Japan for tax purposes for the whole year and still live in Japan on January 1 next year. Foreign income, tax treaties and the rules for non-residents are not handled.

How it is calculated, and the basis

Both deductions are worked out, income tax and resident tax are calculated under each, and the one with less tax is shown.

Medical expense deduction = (medical expenses − reimbursements) − ¥100,000 (or 5% of total income if your total income is under ¥2 million), up to ¥2,000,000

Self-medication deduction = eligible medicines − ¥12,000, up to ¥88,000

  • The deduction for medical expenses covers what you paid during the year for yourself and for a spouse or relatives in the same household. Amounts covered by insurance, the high-cost medical care benefit, the childbirth lump-sum and the like are subtracted (NTA Tax Answer No. 1120: deduction for medical expenses (Japanese only)).
  • The self-medication tax system (セルフメディケーション税制) is only for people who themselves had a health check, vaccination or similar preventive check-up during the year. It covers “switch OTC” medicines and similar; eligible items are marked on the receipt (NTA Tax Answer No. 1129: the self-medication tax system (Japanese only)).
  • You choose one. You cannot combine them, and you cannot switch after filing, not even by amending the return (NTA Tax Answer No. 1129: the self-medication tax system (Japanese only)).
  • Resident tax uses the same formula (Local Tax Act, Art. 314-2 para. 1 item 2 and Supplementary Provisions Art. 4-5 (Japanese only)). The tax saved is roughly the deduction × (income tax rate × 1.021 + 10% resident tax).
  • On the final return, the deduction goes in box ㉘ (医療費) on page 1. If you choose self-medication, write 1 in the “区分” box next to it and attach the self-medication statement (NTA filing guide (tax year 2025): deduction for medical expenses (Japanese only); NTA: draft final income tax return form for tax year 2026 (Japanese only)). The NTA's English income tax guide describes the deduction for medical expenses in English.

Common questions

Can I use both?

No. You choose one, and you cannot change the choice after filing (NTA Tax Answer No. 1129: the self-medication tax system (Japanese only)). The calculator shows the tax under both and points to the lower one.

My medical bills are under ¥100,000. Is there no deduction?

If your total income is under ¥2 million, the threshold is 5% of total income instead of ¥100,000 (NTA Tax Answer No. 1120: deduction for medical expenses (Japanese only)). With the table's assumptions and an income of ¥1,500,000, total income is ¥850,000 and the threshold is ¥42,500. For freelancers, total income is after the blue-return deduction, so the threshold can be lower than revenue suggests.

Which do freelancers usually pick?

In a year without serious illness or injury, medical spending often stays near the threshold and self-medication gives the bigger deduction. With the table's assumptions and an income of ¥5,000,000, the regular deduction wins once medical spending (after reimbursements) exceeds ¥118,000, if you spent ¥30,000 on eligible medicines. Self-medication needs a health check or similar during the year.

How much do I get back?

The deduction times your tax rates. With the table's assumptions, a freelancer with an income of ¥5,000,000 who paid ¥200,000 in medical expenses pays ¥20,200 less income tax and resident tax in total. Freelancers usually have no tax withheld, so nothing is “refunded”; the tax due on the return goes down.

Where does it go on the tax return?

Box ㉘ (医療費) on page 1 of the final return. For self-medication, also write 1 in the “区分” box. Attach the statement for the one you choose (NTA filing guide (tax year 2025): deduction for medical expenses (Japanese only)).
