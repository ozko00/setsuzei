Social insurance premiums

National Health Insurance (kokumin kenko hoken) plus National Pension (kokumin nenkin): the total you pay from January to December this year. All of it is deducted from your income.

Health insurance, Employees' Pension Insurance and employment insurance taken out of your salary: the total for the year. All of it is deducted from your income.

Where do I find this?
  • Employees: the “社会保険料等の金額” box on your withholding slip (源泉徴収票). If this year's slip is not out yet, last year's figure is a fair guide.
  • National Health Insurance: the annual amount on the bill (納付通知書) your city office sends. If you pay by direct debit, add up the debits in your bank book.
  • National Pension: the total of your payment slips, or the deduction certificate (控除証明書) that arrives in autumn.
  • If you filed a tax return last year, the “社会保険料控除” line on page 1 of the return also helps.

Blue or white: how can I tell?
  • If you filed the blue return application (青色申告承認申請書) with the tax office, usually when you opened the business, you file a blue return. If not, you file a white return.
  • Double-entry books kept with accounting software and a return filed online with e-Tax give ¥650,000. The same books filed on paper give ¥550,000. Simple books give ¥100,000.
  • If you have last year's return, the amount is on page 1 in the “青色申告特別控除額” box.

          About this result

          This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and simplified assumptions. Your actual tax depends on what you file and on your municipality. It does not replace the judgment of a tax accountant (zeirishi), the tax office or your city office. Check with a professional or the office in charge before any important decision.

          It assumes you are a resident of Japan for tax purposes for the whole year, that everything you entered is taxable in Japan, and that you still live in Japan on January 1 next year (resident tax is charged by the municipality where you live on that date). Foreign income, tax treaties and the rules for non-residents are not handled.

          Not calculated: the full National Health Insurance premium, enterprise tax, consumption tax, the waiver of the per-person part of resident tax, separately taxed income (such as capital gains), the housing loan credit and other tax credits.

              About this calculator

              From your forecast income, expenses and family for the year, it estimates income tax for tax year 2026 (Reiwa 8) and resident tax for fiscal 2027, and how much these four things would cut them:

              • Furusato nozei (ふるさと納税, “hometown tax”): you donate to a municipality of your choice, get local products in return, and the donation minus about ¥2,000 comes off your income tax and resident tax. The calculator gives the donation limit at which the out-of-pocket cost stays at about ¥2,000, and the tax saved.
              • iDeCo: an individual-type defined contribution pension. Contributions are fully deductible; the money is locked until 60. You get the room left under the annual limit and the tax saved by filling it.
              • Small Business Mutual Aid (小規模企業共済, shokibo kigyo kyosai): a retirement fund for sole proprietors and small-company directors. Contributions of up to ¥70,000 a month are fully deductible. You get the room left and the tax saved.
              • Special deduction for blue returns (青色申告特別控除): ¥100,000, ¥550,000 or ¥650,000 off business income for people approved to file a “blue return” with proper books. You get the tax at each level and the difference.

              The total under “To do before year-end” is recomputed with everything together, not added up. It includes furusato nozei and, for people who are already members, extra Small Business Mutual Aid contributions. Joining Small Business Mutual Aid and extra iDeCo contributions are included only when you enter an amount yourself, because joining in time needs a visit to a counter, and iDeCo amounts can be changed only once a year.

              You can move each amount and see how the tax changes. If you enter what you can spend before year-end, you get the mix that saves the most tax within it. Furusato nozei goes first because it cuts tax by almost as much as you pay, but it is an exchange for a donation: you do not end up with more cash.

              Assumptions

              • Every amount is treated as your forecast for the whole calendar year. Japan's tax year is January to December.
              • You are a resident of Japan for tax purposes all year and still live in Japan on January 1, 2027. Resident tax (住民税, juminzei; “inhabitant tax” in NTA's English guide) for fiscal 2027 is billed from June 2027 by the municipality where you live on that date.
              • Social insurance premiums are deducted as entered. While “I don't know” is selected, a rough figure is used and shown with its range. For employees it comes from the Employees' Pension, Kyokai Kenpo health insurance and employment insurance rates; for freelancers from the flat National Pension premium and the National Health Insurance rates of three cities. Your real figure will differ.
              • With only the first three answers, it assumes no blue-return deduction, no dependents, and no contributions or donations so far, and says so in the result. For “Employee + side income”, the side income is treated as miscellaneous income (you can change this).
              • Resident tax uses the standard rate: 10% on income plus ¥5,000 for the per-person part and the forest environment tax.
              • Furusato nozei assumes you file a final tax return. The iDeCo limit is assumed to be ¥68,000 a month with no salary, or ¥23,000 a month for an employee whose company has no corporate pension (you can change it).
              • The income level below which the income-based part of resident tax is waived is worked out from your spouse and dependents (including children under 16). The waiver of the per-person part differs by municipality and is not calculated.
              • The full National Health Insurance premium, enterprise tax, consumption tax, separately taxed income, and the housing loan credit are not calculated.

              Sources

              Every rate, deduction and limit comes from a primary source: the National Tax Agency's Tax Answers, the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, statutes on e-Gov (such as the Local Tax Act), the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, and SMRJ. The source for each number is listed under “Primary sources behind the numbers” above and in the data file. English terms follow the NTA's English income tax guide where it has one.
