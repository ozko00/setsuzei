Small Business Mutual Aid (shokibo kigyo kyosai) tax savings calculator

Small Business Mutual Aid (小規模企業共済, shokibo kigyo kyosai) is a retirement fund for sole proprietors and directors of small companies, run by SMRJ (the Organization for Small & Medium Enterprises and Regional Innovation, a government agency). You pay ¥1,000 to ¥70,000 a month, deduct all of it from your income, and receive it back when you close the business or retire. SMRJ's own savings table is indexed by taxable income; here you enter revenue and expenses and the calculator works out the rest.

About this result

This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and simplified assumptions. Your actual tax depends on what you file and on your municipality. It does not replace the judgment of a tax accountant (zeirishi), the tax office or your city office. Check with a professional or the office in charge before any important decision.

It assumes you are a resident of Japan for tax purposes for the whole year and still live in Japan on January 1 next year. Foreign income, tax treaties and the rules for non-residents are not handled.

How it is calculated, and the basis

The saving is the difference between your tax without and with the contributions.

Tax saved ≈ contributions × (income tax rate × 1.021 + resident tax 10%)

  • Contributions are ¥1,000 to ¥70,000 a month, in ¥500 steps, and are deducted in full as small business mutual aid premiums. Prepayments covering up to one year ahead are deductible too (SMRJ: Small Business Mutual Aid contributions (Japanese only); NTA Tax Answer No. 1135: deduction for small business mutual aid premiums (Japanese only)).
  • The income tax rate is 5% to 45% depending on taxable income (NTA Tax Answer No. 2260: income tax rates (Japanese only)); the income-based part of resident tax is a flat 10% (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: individual resident tax (Japanese only)).
  • SMRJ publishes a table of savings by taxable income and monthly contribution (SMRJ: Small Business Mutual Aid contributions (Japanese only)). This calculator's engine is tested against that table (tests). The table below applies the higher basic exemption for tax year 2026 and is indexed by revenue minus expenses.
  • Sole proprietors and partners of small businesses and directors of small companies can join. Employees cannot, even if they run a business on the side (SMRJ: who can join Small Business Mutual Aid (Japanese only)). This calculator does not check whether you qualify.
  • The saving comes in exchange for paying the contributions. You get the money back as a benefit when you close the business or retire, and it is taxed then: a lump sum is retirement income, instalments are pension income, and a voluntary cancellation before 65 is occasional income. Cancel voluntarily with fewer than 240 months (20 years) of contributions and you get back less than you paid; with fewer than 12 months you get nothing (SMRJ: claiming Small Business Mutual Aid benefits (Japanese only)). Tax at payout is not estimated here.
  • Once income tax and the income-based part of resident tax reach zero, more contributions save nothing. The calculator stops the extra amount there.

Common questions

How much tax does Small Business Mutual Aid save?

It depends on your income and contributions. With an income of ¥5,000,000 (same assumptions as the table), paying the maximum ¥70,000 a month (¥840,000 a year) cuts income tax and resident tax by ¥158,000 a year in total. The contributions come back to you later as a benefit, which is taxed separately when you receive it (not covered here).

Do I lose money if I quit early?

With a voluntary cancellation, yes: with fewer than 240 months (20 years) of contributions the refund is less than you paid in, and with fewer than 12 months there is no refund at all. The payout is also taxed: a voluntary cancellation before 65 is occasional income, a lump sum on closing the business is retirement income, and instalments are pension income (SMRJ: claiming Small Business Mutual Aid benefits (Japanese only)). Decide on the basis that the money stays in for a long time, not on the tax saving alone.

Can I use both iDeCo and Small Business Mutual Aid?

Yes. Both are deducted from income under the same heading (NTA Tax Answer No. 1135: deduction for small business mutual aid premiums (Japanese only)). If you max out both, your taxable income falls a lot, you may drop to a lower bracket, and the combined saving is smaller than the two calculated separately. The full calculator gives the saving with everything done together.

If I raise my contributions in December, does it count for this year?

Contributions are deducted in the year you pay them, and prepayments covering up to one year ahead are deductible in the year paid (NTA Tax Answer No. 1135: deduction for small business mutual aid premiums (Japanese only); SMRJ: Small Business Mutual Aid contributions (Japanese only)). Check how to raise or prepay, and the cut-off dates, with SMRJ or the bank or business association that handles your account.

Does it lower my National Health Insurance premium?

No. The income-based part of the premium is charged on total income minus the basic deduction, and this deduction is not subtracted (Order for Enforcement of the National Health Insurance Act, Art. 29-7 (Japanese only); Local Tax Act, Art. 703-4 (Japanese only)).
