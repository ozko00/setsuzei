iDeCo tax savings calculator for freelancers and the self-employed

iDeCo is Japan's individual-type defined contribution pension: you pay in monthly, choose the investments, and can take the money out from age 60. Every yen you contribute is deducted from your income. For the self-employed (Category 1 insured persons of the National Pension) the limit is ¥68,000 a month, ¥816,000 a year. About this result

This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and simplified assumptions. Your actual tax depends on what you file and on your municipality. It does not replace the judgment of a tax accountant (zeirishi), the tax office or your city office. Check with a professional or the office in charge before any important decision.

It assumes you are a resident of Japan for tax purposes for the whole year and still live in Japan on January 1 next year. Foreign income, tax treaties and the rules for non-residents are not handled.

How it is calculated, and the basis

The saving is the difference between your tax without and with the contributions.

Tax saved ≈ contributions × (income tax rate × 1.021 + resident tax 10%)

  • iDeCo contributions are deducted in full from income, under the deduction for small business mutual aid premiums (NTA Tax Answer No. 1135: deduction for small business mutual aid premiums (Japanese only)). What iDeCo is and who can join is explained in English on the iDeCo official site.
  • The income tax rate is 5% to 45% depending on taxable income (NTA Tax Answer No. 2260: income tax rates (Japanese only)). If the contributions move you into a lower bracket, the saving is a little smaller than the formula; the calculator accounts for that.
  • The income-based part of resident tax is a flat 10% (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: individual resident tax (Japanese only)). The resident tax saving shows up in the following fiscal year (fiscal 2027).
  • The limit is shared with National Pension Fund contributions and the additional pension premium (iDeCo official site: four points before you start (Japanese only)).
  • The saving comes in exchange for paying the contributions. As a rule you cannot withdraw before 60, and account fees apply, which differ by provider (iDeCo official site: features of iDeCo (Japanese only)).
  • The payout is taxable: a lump sum is retirement income (NTA Tax Answer No. 1420: retirement income (Japanese only)) and an annuity is treated like a public pension, each with its own deduction (iDeCo official site: tax advantages (Japanese only)). This calculator does not estimate tax at payout.
  • Late in the year an increase often comes too late. The monthly amount can be changed only once per contribution year, December to November (iDeCo official site: eligibility, contributions and benefits (Japanese only)), and paying several months at once needs an annual-schedule form filed in advance (National Pension Fund Association: paying contributions on an annual schedule (Japanese only)).
  • Once income tax and the income-based part of resident tax reach zero, more contributions save nothing. The calculator stops the extra amount there.

Common questions

How much tax does iDeCo save a freelancer?

It depends on your income and contributions. With an income of ¥5,000,000 (same assumptions as the table), paying the maximum ¥68,000 a month (¥816,000 a year) cuts income tax and resident tax by ¥154,400 a year in total. The money is basically locked until you turn 60 (iDeCo official site: four points before you start (Japanese only)), the account has fees, and the payout is taxable (iDeCo official site: features of iDeCo (Japanese only), iDeCo official site: tax advantages (Japanese only)). Weigh it against the cash you need on hand.

Can I raise my iDeCo contributions in December to save more this year?

Often not. You can change the amount only once per contribution year, December to the following November (iDeCo official site: eligibility, contributions and benefits (Japanese only)). Paying the remaining room in one go needs an annual-schedule form filed in advance, which takes effect from the month after you apply (debited the month after that), and missed months cannot be made up (National Pension Fund Association: paying contributions on an annual schedule (Japanese only)). Only amounts debited by December (the November contribution) count for this year, so under “Extra you can still pay in this year” enter what your provider confirms you can actually add.

I heard the limit goes up in December 2026. Does that raise my 2026 saving?

No. The limit for Category 1 insured persons rises to ¥75,000 a month from December 2026 (the ministry's notice (Japanese only)), but the December contribution is debited in January of the following year (iDeCo official site: four points before you start (Japanese only); the National Pension Fund Association also says “from the December 2026 contribution, debited in January 2027”, National Pension Fund Association: notice on changing contributions for the higher limit (Japanese only)). Contributions are deducted in the year they are paid, so tax year 2026 (payments from January to December 2026) is calculated at ¥68,000 a month.

Does iDeCo lower my National Health Insurance premium?

No. The income-based part of the premium is charged on total income minus the basic deduction, and deductions such as iDeCo are not subtracted (Order for Enforcement of the National Health Insurance Act, Art. 29-7 (Japanese only); Local Tax Act, Art. 703-4 (Japanese only)). What goes down is income tax and resident tax.

Does iDeCo save tax in a low-income year?

Not if you have no taxable income. With an income of ¥1,200,000 (same assumptions as the table), taxable income for income tax is ¥0 and contributing up to the limit saves ¥0. A deduction can only reduce tax you would otherwise owe.
