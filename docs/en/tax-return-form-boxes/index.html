About this result

This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and simplified assumptions. Your actual tax depends on what you file and on your municipality. It does not replace the judgment of a tax accountant (zeirishi), the tax office or your city office. Check with a professional or the office in charge before any important decision.

It assumes you are a resident of Japan for tax purposes for the whole year and still live in Japan on January 1 next year. Foreign income, tax treaties and the rules for non-residents are not handled.

How it is calculated, and the basis

The calculation is the same as on the other pages; the results are laid out by box on the return.

  • Box numbers and names were checked against the draft final return for tax year 2026 published by the National Tax Agency, pages 1 and 2 (NTA: draft final income tax return form for tax year 2026 (Japanese only)). It is a draft: when the final form is published, the numbers will be checked and replaced.
  • On page 1 of the 2026 form, revenue is in boxes ㋐ onward, income in ① to ⑫, deductions from income in ⑬ to ㉚, and the tax calculation from ㉛ (the tax due is ㊿).
  • Tax withheld (㊾), prepaid tax and tax credits such as the housing loan credit are not calculated. Subtract them to get the tax to pay or the refund.
  • If you file a final return, you do not need a separate resident tax return: the resident tax section on page 2 goes to your municipality. If you do not file one, file a resident tax return with your municipality instead; its form differs by municipality.
  • The deductions for medical expenses (NTA Tax Answer No. 1120: deduction for medical expenses (Japanese only)), self-medication (NTA Tax Answer No. 1129: the self-medication tax system (Japanese only)) and life insurance premiums (NTA Tax Answer No. 1140: deduction for life insurance premiums (Japanese only)) follow their own formulas, including the 2026 rule that raises the life insurance deduction for people with a dependent under 23. The NTA's English income tax guide explains each deduction in English.

Common questions

Do I file both page 1 and page 2?

Yes. Page 1 (第一表) has revenue, income, deductions and tax; page 2 (第二表) has the details: insurance by type, who you donated to, your family, and the resident tax section. From tax year 2026 the carbon copy is no longer included (NTA: draft final income tax return form for tax year 2026 (Japanese only)).

Where does furusato nozei go?

The deduction for donations (total donated − ¥2,000) goes in box ㉙ on page 1, and the municipalities and amounts in the donations section on page 2. Also write the total in “都道府県、市区町村への寄附（特例控除対象）” in the resident tax section on page 2; without it, the special portion of the resident tax credit is not applied (NTA Tax Answer No. 1155: furusato nozei (deduction for donations) (Japanese only); Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: furusato nozei portal, how the deduction works (Japanese only)).

How do I keep resident tax on side income off my salary?

In the resident tax section on page 2, choose 自分で納付 (pay it yourself) under “給与、公的年金等以外の所得に係る住民税の徴収方法”. Some municipalities may not allow it. Resident tax on your salary is still deducted from your pay.

Is there an English version of the return?

The return itself is only in Japanese. The NTA's English income tax guide explains the system and the deductions in English, and the online return preparation on e-Tax fills in the boxes for you. This page maps your amounts to the Japanese boxes.

If I use e-Tax, do I need this page?

e-Tax's online return preparation (確定申告書等作成コーナー) fills in the boxes as you enter amounts. Use this page to estimate the amounts beforehand, or to check the figures on the finished return.
