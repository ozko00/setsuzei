Furusato nozei limit calculator for freelancers and sole proprietors

Furusato nozei (ふるさと納税, “hometown tax”) lets you donate to a Japanese municipality, receive local products in return, and have the donation minus about ¥2,000 taken off your income tax and resident tax. The charts you see online are indexed by an employee's salary; a freelancer's limit depends on income (revenue minus expenses) and deductions. Enter yours to get the donation limit at which the out-of-pocket cost stays at about ¥2,000.

About this result

This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and simplified assumptions. Your actual tax depends on what you file and on your municipality. It does not replace the judgment of a tax accountant (zeirishi), the tax office or your city office. Check with a professional or the office in charge before any important decision.

It assumes you are a resident of Japan for tax purposes for the whole year and still live in Japan on January 1 next year. Foreign income, tax treaties and the rules for non-residents are not handled.

How it is calculated, and the basis

The limit is worked out as follows.

Limit = income-based part of resident tax × 20% ÷ (90% − income tax rate × 1.021) + ¥2,000

  • The furusato nozei deduction has three parts: a deduction for donations in income tax, a basic portion in resident tax (10%), and a special portion in resident tax. The special portion is capped at 20% of the income-based part of your resident tax, and the donation that reaches that cap is the limit at which you pay only ¥2,000 yourself (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: furusato nozei portal, how the deduction works (Japanese only); Local Tax Act, Art. 314-7 and Supplementary Provisions Art. 5-6 (Japanese only)).
  • The income tax rate in the formula is your bracket, 5% to 45% in seven steps, set by your taxable income (NTA Tax Answer No. 2260: income tax rates (Japanese only)). The 1.021 is the special income tax for reconstruction, a 2.1% surcharge on income tax.
  • Resident tax (住民税, juminzei) is the local tax you pay to your city and prefecture. Its income-based part is 10% of your income after deductions, less a small adjustment credit (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: individual resident tax (Japanese only)).
  • Your tax goes down by at most the donation minus ¥2,000 (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: furusato nozei portal, how the deduction works (Japanese only)). The same money leaves your account as a donation, so you do not end up with more cash. What you gain is the return gifts.
  • If your income is low enough that the income-based part of resident tax is waived (total income of ¥350,000 × (spouse and dependents + 1) + ¥100,000 or less, plus ¥320,000 if you have a spouse or dependents; Local Tax Act, Supplementary Provisions Art. 3-3 (Japanese only)), no limit is shown. The same applies just above that level, where the tax is reduced. Children under 16 can be entered in the full calculator.
  • The limit is rounded down to the nearest ¥1,000 and assumes you file a final tax return (kakutei shinkoku). Freelancers file one, so the “one-stop exception” is not available: it is only for employees and others who do not need to file (the ministry's explanation (Japanese only)). How to claim the deduction on the return is in the NTA's English income tax guide under “Deduction for donations”.

Common questions

Does furusato nozei leave me with more money?

No. Your tax goes down by at most the donation minus ¥2,000 (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: furusato nozei portal, how the deduction works (Japanese only)). You pay the donation first and get almost the same amount back later as lower tax, so it works like paying part of your tax in advance. What you gain is the return gifts. Anything you donate above the limit comes out of your own pocket.

Can I use the charts made for employees?

Not reliably. Those charts take a salary, subtract the employment income deduction and assume social insurance at a fixed share of pay. A freelancer's limit comes from revenue minus expenses and the blue-return deduction, so the same “annual income” gives a different limit. Enter your revenue and expenses above.

Does paying into iDeCo or Small Business Mutual Aid change my limit?

Yes, it lowers it. Contributions are fully deductible, which shrinks the income-based part of your resident tax. With an income of ¥5,000,000 and no dependents (same assumptions as the table), the limit is ¥80,000; paying ¥816,000 a year into iDeCo brings it to ¥56,000. Decide your contributions first, then your donations.

I can't predict my revenue until year-end. How much is safe?

The limit follows your income. If income comes in below your forecast, the limit drops and the excess is out of pocket. The row one step below your expected income in the table gives a feel for the downside. The surest way is to enter a cautious revenue figure before you donate.

With the same income as last year, is my 2026 limit different?

It can be. From tax year 2026 the basic exemption for income tax is higher (up to ¥1,040,000 depending on total income; NTA Tax Answer No. 1199: basic exemption (Japanese only), NTA's notice on the change (Japanese only)). That lowers taxable income and moves some people into a lower bracket. A lower rate makes the denominator of the formula larger, so the limit goes down a little. Your income tax itself is lower by the larger exemption.
