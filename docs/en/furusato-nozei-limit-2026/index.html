Is your furusato nozei limit lower for 2026? Compare it with 2025

For tax year 2026 (donations made during 2026) the limit is lower than for 2025 in some income bands, because the basic exemption for income tax went up. For most people it is unchanged, and at the same income it goes up for no one. For example, a single person with a ¥5,000,000 salary goes from ¥61,000 to ¥57,000, while at ¥6,000,000 it stays at ¥77,000 (assuming social insurance at 15% of salary). About this result

This is an estimate based on the figures you entered and simplified assumptions. Your actual tax depends on what you file and on your municipality. It does not replace the judgment of a tax accountant (zeirishi), the tax office or your city office. Check with a professional or the office in charge before any important decision.

It assumes you are a resident of Japan for tax purposes for the whole year and still live in Japan on January 1 next year. Foreign income, tax treaties and the rules for non-residents are not handled.

How it is calculated, and the basis

The short answer first. For tax year 2026 the limit goes down only if one of the following applies to you. Otherwise, with the same income and deductions, it is the same as for 2025. In no case does it go up at the same income and deductions (0 cases in the scan below).

  • You were just above one of the thresholds that set the rate of the special portion (¥0, ¥1.95 million, ¥3.3 million, ¥6.95 million, ¥9 million, ¥18 million). For a single person with social insurance at 15% of salary, that means a salary of ¥4.76 million to ¥5.27 million (by up to ¥4,000); ¥6.71 million to ¥6.76 million (by up to ¥13,000); ¥11.16 million to ¥11.2 million (by up to ¥10,000); ¥13.57 million to ¥13.61 million (by up to ¥50,000); ¥24.16 million to ¥24.2 million (by up to ¥94,000).
  • Your salary is under ¥2.2 million. The minimum employment income deduction rose from ¥650,000 to ¥740,000 (NTA Tax Answer No. 1410: employment income deduction (Japanese only)), which lowers the income-based part of resident tax. Because the limit is rounded down to ¥1,000, it sometimes stays the same.

The formula

Limit = income-based part of resident tax × 20% ÷ rate of the special portion + ¥2,000

  • Of the resident tax credit for furusato nozei, the “special portion” is capped at 20% of the income-based part of your resident tax. The donation that reaches the cap is the limit at which you pay about ¥2,000 yourself (Local Tax Act, Art. 314-7 paras. 1 and 11; Art. 37-2 for prefectural tax (Japanese only); Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: furusato nozei portal, how the deduction works (Japanese only)).
  • The rate of the special portion is 90% − income tax rate × 1.021, in steps: 84.895% (5% income tax), 79.79% (10%), 69.58% (20%) and so on (the table in item 1 of the same paragraph, and Supplementary Provisions Art. 5-6).
  • Which step applies is set by your resident-tax taxable income minus the “personal-deduction adjustment” (item 1 of the same paragraph).

What changed

  • Act No. 2 of 2026 amended Art. 314-7 para. 11 item 1 (Art. 37-2 para. 11 item 1 for prefectural tax) so that the personal-deduction adjustment now includes the basic exemption for income tax minus ¥480,000 (zero if negative). It took effect on April 1, 2026 and applies from fiscal 2026 resident tax, that is, from donations made in 2025 (the amended text and Supplementary Provisions Art. 11 para. 1 (Japanese only); Ministry of Finance: commentary on the FY2026 tax reform, Local Tax Act (pp. 931-932) (Japanese only)). Only the income tax exemption was raised, while the resident tax basic deduction stayed at ¥430,000, so the way the rate is chosen was adjusted to match the income tax rate people actually pay.
  • The formula changed for 2025. The further difference for 2026 comes from the income tax basic exemption rising again (NTA Tax Answer No. 1199: basic exemption (Japanese only)): for total income of ¥4.89 million or less, from ¥880,000 or ¥680,000 (¥950,000 at ¥1.32 million or less) to ¥1,040,000; above ¥4.89 million up to ¥6.55 million, from ¥630,000 to ¥670,000; above ¥6.55 million up to ¥23.5 million, from ¥580,000 to ¥620,000. A larger subtraction lowers the amount that picks the rate, and some people move down one income tax bracket.
  • One bracket lower means a higher rate for the special portion (for example 79.79% → 84.895%). At a higher rate the same donation produces a larger special portion, so the cap (20% of the income-based part) is reached with a smaller donation. That is why the limit goes down.
  • The income-based part of resident tax itself is unchanged for salaries of ¥2.2 million or more. The resident tax basic deduction, spouse and dependent deductions and the adjustment credit are the same provisions in both years (Local Tax Act, Arts. 314-2 and 314-6).

Common questions

Does everyone's furusato nozei limit go down for 2026?

No, only in some income bands. For a single person on salary only with social insurance at 15%, scanning salaries from ¥1 million to ¥25 million in steps of ¥10,000 gives 2,401 cases: 150 lower, 2,251 unchanged and 0 higher. A single person with a ¥6,000,000 salary, for example, stays at ¥77,000.

Why would a higher basic exemption lower anyone's limit?

The limit is the income-based part of resident tax × 20% ÷ the rate of the special portion + ¥2,000, and that rate is higher when your income tax rate is lower (90% − income tax rate × 1.021). When the higher exemption moves you down one income tax bracket, the rate goes up and the 20% cap is reached with a smaller donation. The resident tax basic deduction is still ¥430,000, so the income-based part does not grow. The rule that brings the income tax exemption into the choice of rate is the amendment of Local Tax Act Art. 314-7 para. 11 item 1 by Act No. 2 of 2026 (e-Gov: Local Tax Act (text in force from April 1, 2026) (Japanese only); Ministry of Finance: commentary on the FY2026 tax reform, Local Tax Act (pp. 931-932) (Japanese only)).

Does a lower limit mean I am worse off?

Only the amount you can donate at ¥2,000 out of pocket goes down; your tax burden does not go up. The limit falls for people whose income tax fell and whose bracket dropped. In the ¥5,000,000, single example, donating the 2026 limit of ¥57,000 costs ¥2,000 out of pocket (donation minus the income tax and resident tax saved, by this calculator's 2026 calculation). Furusato nozei cuts tax by the donation minus the out-of-pocket amount; it does not leave you with more cash (Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications: furusato nozei portal, how the deduction works (Japanese only)).

What happens if I donate up to my 2025 limit?

Above the limit the special portion stops growing and the excess is out of pocket. In the ¥5,000,000, single example, donating the 2025 limit of ¥61,000 during 2026 costs ¥5,000 out of pocket, against ¥2,000 at the 2026 limit of ¥57,000. Recalculate with this year's forecast rather than reusing a chart or last year's amount.

The ministry's chart says ¥61,000 for a ¥5,000,000 salary, single. Which is right?

The ministry's chart was made under the rules before the basic exemption was raised. Up to tax year 2025 the limit under these assumptions is ¥61,000, the same as the chart. For tax year 2026 (donations made in 2026) the provisions give ¥57,000 (Local Tax Act, Art. 314-7 para. 11 item 1 (Japanese only); NTA Tax Answer No. 1199: basic exemption (Japanese only)). We could not find an official chart that reflects 2026, so the figures here are calculated from the law. You can confirm the real amount on the resident tax notice that arrives around June of the following year.

Why does it go down for salaries under ¥2.2 million?

Because the minimum employment income deduction rose from ¥650,000 for 2025 to ¥740,000 for 2026 (NTA Tax Answer No. 1410: employment income deduction (Japanese only)). Lower employment income means a lower income-based part of resident tax, and the cap on the special portion is 20% of that. A single person with a ¥2,000,000 salary (social insurance ¥300,000) goes from ¥15,000 to ¥13,000.

From which donations does this apply?

Donations made from January 1 to December 31, 2026 belong to tax year 2026 and are deducted from 2026 income tax and fiscal 2027 resident tax. The higher basic exemption takes effect on December 1, 2026 but applies to the whole of tax year 2026 (NTA: the 2026 increase of the basic exemption for income tax (Japanese only); NTA Tax Answer No. 1199: basic exemption (Japanese only)).
